# Concept and research frame

## Central hypothesis

A data center can be designed as part of a restored ecological-industrial system rather than as an isolated building. The facility supplies controlled heat, condensate, electricity-management capability, data, and funding. The surrounding landscape supplies shade, stormwater regulation, biological water polishing, carbon storage, habitat, and a moderated microclimate.

The relationship is potentially symbiotic, but not automatically so. A forest cannot cancel heat thermodynamically, and trees must not become a license to emit carbon or consume water. Every claimed benefit needs a measurable mechanism, baseline, and boundary.

## Candidate physical loops

### Energy and heat

- Direct-to-chip warm-water cooling captures heat at a useful temperature.
- Heat pumps raise temperature for crop drying, sanitation, or controlled agriculture.
- Thermal storage shifts heat rejection away from the hottest hours.
- Compute scheduling follows renewable availability and local thermal constraints.
- Recovered heat can dry responsibly sourced biochar feedstock.

### Water

- Closed cooling loops minimize routine withdrawals.
- Roof and hardscape runoff feeds storage after first-flush diversion.
- Air-handler condensate is recovered.
- Constructed wetlands polish suitable non-hazardous wastewater.
- Discharge targets ecological flow, temperature, and chemistry—not merely legal minimums.

### Carbon, soil, and habitat

- Restore native forest on previously degraded land.
- Apply responsibly sourced biochar where field evidence supports it.
- Use soil cover, swales, and diverse root systems to reduce erosion and increase infiltration.
- Measure carbon with conservative permanence and leakage assumptions.
- Connect habitat while controlling light, noise, construction traffic, and invasive species.

## Siting screen

Reject sites requiring intact-forest clearing, threatening protected habitat, competing for scarce community water, increasing flood risk, lacking credible clean energy, or proceeding without legitimate local consent.

Prefer degraded agricultural or industrial land near fiber and power infrastructure, with enough surrounding area for restoration and water management.

## Measurement layer

Suggested instrumentation includes electrical submeters, water-flow and quality sensors, inlet/outlet heat meters, weather stations, soil probes, groundwater wells, acoustic monitoring, camera traps, vegetation plots, and independent carbon and biodiversity audits.

## Pilot success criteria

1. No conversion of intact natural habitat.
2. Declining life-cycle emissions on a science-aligned trajectory.
3. Non-depleting watershed operation under drought scenarios.
4. No harmful increase in receiving-environment temperature.
5. Independently verified native habitat and soil gains.
6. Durable, documented benefits for neighboring communities.
